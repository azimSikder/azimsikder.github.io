## Undergraduate Thesis

**Quantum Token for Anti-Counterfeit Systems**  
Supervisor: Dr. Sajid Muhaimin Choudhury, Professor, Department of EEE, BUET  
April 2025 – June 2026

Quantum tokens use the laws of quantum mechanics to create credentials that cannot be copied, making them a promising tool against counterfeiting. However, purely quantum schemes need millions of qubits to be secure, far beyond what today's noisy quantum hardware can provide.

This thesis developed a hybrid quantum-classical authentication protocol that combines quantum encoding with a post-quantum cryptographic method, along with a verification approach that can tell hardware noise apart from tampering. The protocol was implemented in Qiskit and tested both in simulation and on the 156-qubit IBM Fez quantum processor.

The hybrid protocol accepted every valid user and rejected every attacker using only 1,500 qubits, compared with the roughly 4.2 million qubits a purely quantum token would require.
